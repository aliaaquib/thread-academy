## How ions form

Atoms can become more stable by gaining or losing electrons. Metal atoms lose outer electrons to form positive ions. Non-metal atoms gain electrons to form negative ions. A sodium atom, 2,8,1, loses one electron to form Na⁺. A chlorine atom, 2,8,7, gains that electron to form Cl⁻.

<Definition term="ion">
  An atom or group of atoms with an electrical charge.
</Definition>

<Definition term="ionic bond">
  The strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
</Definition>

## Giant ionic lattices

Ionic compounds form a regular giant lattice. Strong forces act in all directions, so a lot of energy is needed to break them. This gives high melting points. Solid ionic compounds do not conduct electricity because their ions cannot move. When molten or dissolved, the ions can move and carry charge.

<WorkedExample title="Formula of magnesium oxide">
  Magnesium is in Group II and oxygen is in Group VI. Find the formula of magnesium oxide.

  Mg loses 2 electrons to form Mg²⁺.

  O gains 2 electrons to form O²⁻.

  The charges balance with one of each ion.

  **Answer:** MgO
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Electrons transfer">
  In ionic bonding electrons are transferred. In covalent bonding electrons are shared.
</ImportantNote>

<Summary>
  - Metals lose electrons and non-metals gain electrons.
  - Oppositely charged ions attract strongly.
  - Ionic compounds have high melting points.
  - They conduct when molten or dissolved, not when solid.
</Summary>
