## Counting particles

Atoms are too small to count one by one, so chemists count them in moles. One mole of any substance contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles. This number is called the Avogadro constant. A mole works like the word dozen, but it represents a far larger number.

<Definition term="mole">
  The amount of substance containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles.
</Definition>

<Definition term="molar mass">
  The mass of one mole of a substance, measured in g/mol.
</Definition>

## Moles and mass

The molar mass of a substance has the same value as its relative formula mass. Carbon dioxide has Mr = 44, so one mole has a mass of 44 g.

<Formula label="Moles from mass">
  moles = mass ÷ molar mass
</Formula>

<WorkedExample title="Reacting masses">
  What mass of magnesium oxide forms from 4.8 g of magnesium?

  ```maths
  2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
  moles of Mg = 4.8 ÷ 24 = 0.20 mol
  mole ratio Mg : MgO = 2 : 2
  moles of MgO = 0.20 mol
  mass of MgO = 0.20 × 40 = 8.0 g
  ```

  **Answer:** 8.0 g
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Use the equation ratio">
  Convert to moles before using the ratio. Masses do not follow the balancing numbers directly.
</ImportantNote>

<ExamFocus
  items={[
    { good: "Moles of CO₂ = 11 ÷ 44 = 0.25 mol", bad: "11 × 44 = 484 mol", note: "Divide mass by molar mass." },
  ]}
  mistake="Using the mass ratio from the equation before converting masses into moles."

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="How many moles are in 36 g of water? Mr = 18." answer="36 ÷ 18 = 2.0 mol." />
  <PracticeItem question="Find the mass of 0.50 mol of NaCl. Mr = 58.5." answer="0.50 × 58.5 = 29.25 g." />
  <PracticeItem question="How many atoms are in 2.0 mol of helium?" answer="2.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²⁴ atoms." />
  <PracticeItem question="Calcium carbonate decomposes: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. Find the mass of CaO from 25 g of CaCO₃." answer="Moles of CaCO₃ = 25 ÷ 100 = 0.25 mol. The ratio is 1:1, so mass of CaO = 0.25 × 56 = 14 g." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - One mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles.
  - Moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  - Equation ratios compare moles, not masses.
  - Convert back to mass at the end.
</Summary>
