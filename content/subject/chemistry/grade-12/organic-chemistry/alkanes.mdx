## A saturated family

Alkanes are a homologous series of saturated hydrocarbons. Every carbon-carbon bond is a single bond, so each carbon has as many hydrogen atoms as possible. As chains grow longer, the boiling point rises because forces between molecules increase.

<Definition term="homologous series">
  A family of compounds with the same general formula and similar chemical properties.
</Definition>

<Definition term="saturated">
  Containing only single bonds between carbon atoms.
</Definition>

<Formula label="General formula of alkanes">
  CnH2n+2
</Formula>

## Naming alkanes

The first four alkanes are methane, ethane, propane and butane. Their names show the number of carbon atoms: meth is 1, eth is 2, prop is 3 and but is 4.

## Reactions of alkanes

Alkanes are generally unreactive, but they burn well. Complete combustion in plenty of oxygen forms carbon dioxide and water. Limited oxygen can produce carbon monoxide, which is toxic. In ultraviolet light, a halogen can replace a hydrogen atom in a substitution reaction.

<WorkedExample title="Complete combustion of propane">
  Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane.

  ```maths
  C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
  balance carbon: 3CO₂
  balance hydrogen: 4H₂O
  balance oxygen: 5O₂
  C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
  ```

  **Answer:** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="UV light is required">
  Alkanes react with chlorine by substitution only in the presence of ultraviolet light.
</ImportantNote>
