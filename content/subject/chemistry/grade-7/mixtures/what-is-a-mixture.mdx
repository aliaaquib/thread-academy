## Mixtures are all around us

Air, sea water and a bowl of cereal are mixtures. A mixture contains two or more substances that are together but not chemically joined. Each substance keeps its own properties. Because no new substance forms, we can usually separate the parts again using physical methods.

<Definition term="mixture">
  Two or more substances mixed together but not chemically joined.
</Definition>

<Definition term="pure substance">
  A material that contains only one type of substance.
</Definition>

## Separating mixtures

The best method depends on the properties of the parts. Sieving separates solids with different particle sizes. Filtering separates an undissolved solid from a liquid. Evaporation leaves a dissolved solid behind when the liquid turns to gas.

<WorkedExample title="Sand and salt water">
  A beaker contains sand, salt and water. How can you collect the sand and salt?

  Filter the mixture. The sand stays in the filter paper, while the salty water passes through.

  Heat the salty water. The water evaporates and salt remains.

  **Answer:** Filter first, then evaporate.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Pure can mean two things">
  In everyday life, pure can mean natural or clean. In science, it means containing only one substance.
</ImportantNote>

<Summary>
  - A mixture contains substances that are not chemically joined.
  - Each part keeps its own properties.
