## Key ideas

### Comparing measures

Words like longer, heavier and holds more only make sense when you compare two things directly — always name both sides of the comparison.

### Measuring length

Longer things cover more distance, and we measure length with centimetres and metres using a ruler. Always line up the 0 mark with one end.

**Key term — Capacity:** How much a container can hold, measured in millilitres or litres.

<WorkedExample title="Sorting the toy box">
  A toy box holds 12 triangles, 8 squares and 5 circles. How many more triangles than squares are there, and what is the total of all three shapes?

  More triangles than squares: 12 − 8 = 4.
  Total shapes: 12 + 8 + 5 = 25.

  **Answer:** 4 more triangles; 25 shapes altogether.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Calling every four-sided shape a square** A square needs four equal sides and four right angles; a rectangle has opposite sides equal instead.
  - **Reading the wrong end of the ruler** Always start measuring from the 0 mark, not from the end of the ruler.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A jug holds 500 ml. You pour in 250 ml. How much more can it hold?" hint="Subtract what is already in the jug.">
    250 ml — 500 − 250 = 250.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Order these by weight, lightest first: an apple, a bag of flour, a grape." hint="Picture holding each one.">
    Grape, apple, bag of flour — from lightest to heaviest.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="How many sides does a hexagon have?" hint="Think of the prefix hex-.">
    6 — a hexagon has six sides and six vertices.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is longer: 1 metre or 90 centimetres?" hint="How many centimetres are in a metre?">
    1 metre — it equals 100 cm, which is more than 90 cm.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
