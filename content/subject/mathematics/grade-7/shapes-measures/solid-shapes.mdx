## What makes a shape solid?

A flat shape has length and width. A solid has length, width and height, so it takes up space. A box, a tin of beans and a football are all solids. To describe a solid exactly, we count three things instead of relying on how it looks.

<Definition term="face">
  A flat or curved surface of a solid.
</Definition>

<Definition term="edge">
  A line where two faces meet.
</Definition>

<Definition term="vertex">
  A corner where edges meet. The plural is vertices.
</Definition>

## Common solids

| Solid | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cube | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Cuboid | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square-based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |

A cylinder has 2 flat faces and 1 curved face. A cone has 1 flat face, 1 curved face and a point called the apex. A sphere has 1 curved face and no edges.

## Nets

A net is a flat shape that folds up to make a solid. A cube's net is made of 6 squares. Every face of the solid appears exactly once in its net, so counting the faces is a quick way to check a net.

<WorkedExample title="Describe a triangular prism">
  A tent has the shape of a triangular prism. How many faces, edges and vertices does it have?

  The two ends are triangles, which gives 2 faces. The sides are 3 rectangles, so there are 5 faces.

  Each triangle has 3 edges, and 3 more edges join the triangles, so there are 9 edges.

  Each triangle has 3 corners, so there are 6 vertices.

  **Answer:** 5 faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Faces are not only flat">
  A cylinder has curved surfaces as well as flat ones. Count the curved surface as a face, and say that it is curved.
</ImportantNote>
