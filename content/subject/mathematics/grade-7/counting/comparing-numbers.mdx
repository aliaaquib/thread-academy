This lesson focuses on Comparing Numbers: use more than and fewer than to compare two groups.

<Definition term="Comparing Numbers">
  Use more than and fewer than to compare two groups.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Number order

Numbers grow by one each step, so a number further along in the count is always larger: 9 comes after 7 and is bigger.

### One-to-one counting

Touch or point to each object once and say one number name each time. That way no object is missed and none is counted twice.

**Key term — Digit:** Any of the symbols 0 to 9 used to write numbers. The number 42 uses two digits.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Comparing Numbers">
  Write the numbers 8, 3, 11, 5 from smallest to largest.

  3, 5, 8, 11.

  **Answer:** 3, 5, 8, 11.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Thinking 9 is bigger than 11** Compare by seeing which comes later in the count, not by how big the digit looks.
  - **Saying number names out of order** Practise the sequence aloud until it is automatic, like the words of a song.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What number comes just before 40?" hint="Count backwards from 40.">
    39.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Count from 14 to 20." hint="Say each number one after another.">
    14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is more: 25 or 29?" hint="Which comes later in the count?">
    29, because 29 comes after 25 when counting.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A packet has 31 biscuits. You eat 1. How many are left?" hint="Count back one step.">
    30 — counting back one from 31 gives 30.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
