This lesson focuses on Counting to 100: count on and back in ones and tens up to 100.

<Definition term="Counting to 100">
  Count on and back in ones and tens up to 100.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### One-to-one counting

Touch or point to each object once and say one number name each time. That way no object is missed and none is counted twice.

### Counting on

Instead of starting at 1 each time, keep counting from a number you already know. This makes adding and checking totals much faster.

**Key term — Estimate:** A sensible guess of how many there are, found by counting part of a group and scaling up.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Counting to 100">
  A packet has 31 biscuits. You eat 1. How many are left?

  30 — counting back one from 31 gives 30.

  **Answer:** 30 — counting back one from 31 gives 30.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Counting an object twice** Point to each object once as you count it, moving counted objects aside if it helps.
  - **Thinking 9 is bigger than 11** Compare by seeing which comes later in the count, not by how big the digit looks.
</ImportantNote>
