Adding joins things together and subtracting takes them away. These two ideas are the engine of almost all early mathematics.

This lesson focuses on Adding Two Numbers: combine two groups and count them all to find the total.

<Definition term="Adding Two Numbers">
  Combine two groups and count them all to find the total.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Counting all

Put two groups together and count everything from the start to find the total. This works well with small numbers and real objects like beads or blocks.

### Counting on and back

Start from the first number and count on for addition, or count back for subtraction. This is much quicker than counting everything from 1 each time.

**Key term — Sum:** The answer when you add numbers together. The sum of 4 and 6 is 10.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Adding Two Numbers">
  7 + 5 = ?

  12 — start at 7, then count on: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12.

  **Answer:** 12 — start at 7, then count on: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Forgetting to count on from the bigger number** Start at the larger number and count on the smaller jump to save steps and avoid slips.
  - **Writing 3 + 4 as 34** The + sign means join the amounts into one total of 7, not write the digits side by side.
</ImportantNote>
