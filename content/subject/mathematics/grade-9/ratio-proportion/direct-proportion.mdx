## Key ideas

### Direct proportion

When one quantity doubles, the other doubles too — the ratio between them never changes. Recipes, currency and map scales all work this way.

### Simplifying ratios

Divide every part by the same number, just like cancelling fractions: 10:15 simplifies to 2:3. A ratio in simplest form is easiest to read.

**Key term — Ratio:** A comparison of two or more quantities, written with a colon, such as 3:2.

<WorkedExample title="The recipe">
  A recipe for 4 pancakes uses 200 g flour and 300 ml milk. How much flour and milk are needed for 10 pancakes?

  Find the scale factor: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5.
  Scale the flour: 200 × 2.5 = 500 g.
  Scale the milk: 300 × 2.5 = 750 ml.

  **Answer:** 500 g flour and 750 ml milk.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Adding instead of multiplying when scaling** Doubling a recipe means multiply by 2, not add 2 — scaling is always multiplication.
  - **Simplifying only one part of a ratio** Every part must be divided by the same number, or the comparison changes.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Simplify 12:18." hint="Divide by the highest common factor.">
    2:3 — both divide by 6.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Share £40 in the ratio 3:2." hint="How many shares in total?">
    £24 and £16 — 5 shares of £8 each.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="5 notebooks cost £7.50. What do 8 cost?" hint="Find the cost of one first.">
    £12 — one notebook costs £1.50, and 8 × £1.50 = £12.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A map scale is 1:50,000. A road measures 6 cm on the map. What is its real length in km?" hint="Convert centimetres to kilometres.">
    3 km — 6 × 50,000 = 300,000 cm = 3 km.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
