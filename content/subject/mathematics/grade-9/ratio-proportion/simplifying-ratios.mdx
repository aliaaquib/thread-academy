Ratios compare quantities and proportion keeps them in step — from mixing paint to reading maps, they describe how things scale together.

This lesson focuses on Simplifying Ratios: reduce ratios to their simplest form like fractions.

<Definition term="Simplifying Ratios">
  Reduce ratios to their simplest form like fractions.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Simplifying ratios

Divide every part by the same number, just like cancelling fractions: 10:15 simplifies to 2:3. A ratio in simplest form is easiest to read.

### Sharing in a ratio

Add the parts to find the total number of shares, then divide the amount by that total. For £30 in the ratio 2:3, there are 5 shares of £6.

**Key term — Proportion:** A statement that two ratios are equal, such as 1:2 = 2:4.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Simplifying Ratios">
  Are the ratios 4:6 and 10:15 equivalent?

  Yes — both simplify to 2:3.

  **Answer:** Yes — both simplify to 2:3.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Simplifying only one part of a ratio** Every part must be divided by the same number, or the comparison changes.
  - **Adding instead of multiplying when scaling** Doubling a recipe means multiply by 2, not add 2 — scaling is always multiplication.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Share £40 in the ratio 3:2." hint="How many shares in total?">
    £24 and £16 — 5 shares of £8 each.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A drink mixes juice and water in the ratio 1:4. How much juice is in 500 ml of drink?" hint="Total parts = 5.">
    100 ml — 500 ÷ 5 = 100.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Simplify 12:18." hint="Divide by the highest common factor.">
    2:3 — both divide by 6.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="5 notebooks cost £7.50. What do 8 cost?" hint="Find the cost of one first.">
    £12 — one notebook costs £1.50, and 8 × £1.50 = £12.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
