## The balance idea

An equation says that two expressions are equal. Think of the equals sign as the middle of a balanced scale. If you change one side, you must change the other side in the same way, or the scale tips. This rule lets us remove everything around the unknown until it stands alone.

<Definition term="solving an equation">
  Finding the value of the unknown that makes the equation true.
</Definition>

<Definition term="inverse operation">
  The operation that undoes another. Subtraction undoes addition and division undoes multiplication.
</Definition>

## One-step equations

In `x + 5 = 12`, the `+ 5` is in the way. Undo it by subtracting 5 from both sides:

```maths
x + 5 = 12
x = 12 − 5
x = 7
```

## Two-step equations

`3x + 2 = 14` has two operations on `x`: multiply by 3, then add 2. Undo them in reverse order.

<WorkedExample title="Solve a two-step equation">
  Solve `3x + 2 = 14`.

  ```maths
  3x + 2 = 14
  3x = 12        subtract 2 from both sides
  x = 4          divide both sides by 3
  ```

  **Check:** 3 × 4 + 2 = 14, so the answer is correct.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Always check">
  Put your answer back into the original equation. It takes seconds and catches most mistakes.
</ImportantNote>

<Summary>
  - Solving means finding the value that makes the equation true.
  - Do the same thing to both sides.
  - Undo operations in reverse order.
  - Check by substituting your answer.
</Summary>
