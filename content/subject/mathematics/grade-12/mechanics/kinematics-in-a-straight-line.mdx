Mechanics is mathematics with muscles: it predicts how real objects move. The same equations that time a sprinter's finish also guide satellites — learn them once and the universe starts making sense.

This lesson focuses on Kinematics in a straight line: displacement, velocity and acceleration, with the constant-acceleration SUVAT equations.

<Definition term="Kinematics in a straight line">

</Definition>

## Key ideas

### SUVAT equations

For constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², and v² = u² + 2as. Choose the equation containing the quantities you know and the one unknown you need; always define a positive direction first.

### Projectile motion

Horizontal and vertical motion are independent: horizontal velocity stays constant (no air resistance), while vertical motion accelerates at g = 9.8 m/s² downwards. Resolve the initial velocity into components, then treat each direction with SUVAT.

**Key term — Displacement:** Distance measured in a straight line from a starting point, with direction included; unlike distance, it can be negative.

<WorkedExample title="Constant acceleration">
  A car accelerates uniformly from 4 m/s to 12 m/s in 4 s. Find the acceleration and the distance travelled.

  Acceleration a = (v − u)/t = (12 − 4)/4 = 8/4 = 2 m/s².
  Distance via s = (u + v)/2 × t = (4 + 12)/2 × 4 = 8 × 4 = 32 m.
  Check with s = ut + ½at² = 16 + 16 = 32 m. ✓.

  **Answer:** Acceleration 2 m/s²; distance 32 m.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Confusing speed with velocity, or distance with displacement** SUVAT uses vectors: fix a positive direction at the start and keep signs consistent, or the equations give nonsense.
  - **Using F = ma with the wrong force** It is the resultant (net) force that equals ma — add all forces vectorially first, including friction and weight components.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A particle starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 5 s. Find its final velocity." hint="v = u + at.">
    v = 0 + 3 × 5 = 15 m/s.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A 2 kg mass experiences a resultant force of 10 N. Find its acceleration." hint="F = ma.">
    a = 10/2 = 5 m/s².
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Find the distance travelled in the previous question." hint="s = ut + ½at².">
    s = 0 + ½ × 3 × 25 = 37.5 m.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A box on rough ground has normal reaction 50 N and μ = 0.4. Find the maximum friction." hint="F_max = μR.">
    F_max = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
