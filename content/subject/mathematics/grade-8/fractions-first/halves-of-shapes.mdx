Fractions describe parts of a whole — half a pizza, a quarter of an hour. Splitting things fairly is where fractions begin.

This lesson focuses on Halves of Shapes: split shapes into two equal parts and shade one half.

<Definition term="Halves of Shapes">
  Split shapes into two equal parts and shade one half.
</Definition>

## Key ideas

### Equal parts

A fraction only makes sense if the parts are exactly equal — two uneven pieces are not halves, no matter how they look.

### Halves of numbers

Sharing equally between two people gives each person half, so half of 12 is 6. Finding halves is really division by 2.

**Key term — Half:** One of two equal parts of a whole. Two halves make the whole again.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Halves of Shapes">
  A rectangle is split into 4 equal parts. How many parts make one quarter?

  1 — a quarter is 1 out of 4 equal parts.

  **Answer:** 1 — a quarter is 1 out of 4 equal parts.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Calling unequal pieces halves** The pieces must be exactly the same size for halves, quarters or any fraction to be correct.
  - **Writing the fraction upside down** The total number of equal parts goes on the bottom, not the top.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What is half of 18?" hint="Share 18 between two.">
    9 — 18 ÷ 2 = 9.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Which is greater: 1/2 or 1/4?" hint="Draw both on identical circles.">
    1/2 — two parts make bigger pieces than four.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Half of a class of 24 children are girls. How many girls?" hint="Half means divide by 2.">
    12 — 24 ÷ 2 = 12.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="A quarter of 20 marbles are red. How many are red?" hint="A quarter means divide by 4.">
    5 — 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
