This lesson focuses on Place Value: read, write and compare large numbers using place value.

## Key ideas

### Place value

Each digit's value depends on its position: in 5,042 the 5 is worth five thousand and the 4 is worth forty. Zeros hold the other digits in place.

### Equivalent forms

1/2 = 0.5 = 50% — fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same amount. Choosing the handiest form makes each calculation easier.

**Key term — Prime number:** A whole number greater than 1 with exactly two factors: 1 and itself, such as 7 or 13.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Place Value">
  Write 3/8 as a decimal.

  0.375 — 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

  **Answer:** 0.375 — 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Reading 3.45 as bigger than 3.5** Compare the tenths first: 3.5 is 3.50, which beats 3.45.
  - **Dropping a decimal place** 10% of 40 is 4, not 40 — divide by 10 to find ten per cent.
</ImportantNote>

    7.85 — the third digit, 6, rounds the 4 up to 5.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Work out 2 + 3 × 4." hint="Multiply before you add.">
    14 — 3 × 4 = 12, then 2 + 12 = 14.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is 25% of 80?" hint="25% is one quarter.">
    20 — 80 ÷ 4 = 20.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Work out −4 + 9 − 3." hint="Move along the number line.">
    2 — −4 + 9 = 5, then 5 − 3 = 2.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
