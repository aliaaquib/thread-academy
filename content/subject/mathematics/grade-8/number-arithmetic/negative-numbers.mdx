## Key ideas

### BIDMAS

Without an agreed order, 2 + 3 × 4 could be 20 or 14. BIDMAS — brackets, indices, division and multiplication, addition and subtraction — fixes it as 14.

### Place value

Each digit's value depends on its position: in 5,042 the 5 is worth five thousand and the 4 is worth forty. Zeros hold the other digits in place.

**Key term — BIDMAS:** The agreed order for calculations: Brackets, Indices, Division and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction.

<WorkedExample title="The shopping bill">
  A shopper buys 3 packs of pens at £2.40 each and a notebook for £5. There is a 10% discount on the total. How much is paid?

  Multiply first: 3 × £2.40 = £7.20.
  Add the notebook: £7.20 + £5 = £12.20.
  Find the discount: 10% of £12.20 = £1.22.
  Subtract: £12.20 − £1.22 = £10.98.

  **Answer:** £10.98.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Adding before multiplying** In 2 + 3 × 4 the answer is 14, not 20 — multiplication comes before addition in BIDMAS.
  - **Reading 3.45 as bigger than 3.5** Compare the tenths first: 3.5 is 3.50, which beats 3.45.
</ImportantNote>

    14 — 3 × 4 = 12, then 2 + 12 = 14.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Write 3/8 as a decimal." hint="Divide 3 by 8.">
    0.375 — 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="What is 25% of 80?" hint="25% is one quarter.">
    20 — 80 ÷ 4 = 20.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Work out −4 + 9 − 3." hint="Move along the number line.">
    2 — −4 + 9 = 5, then 5 − 3 = 2.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
