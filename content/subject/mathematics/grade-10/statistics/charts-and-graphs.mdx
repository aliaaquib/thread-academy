## Key ideas

### Reading charts carefully

Check the axes first: a bar chart that starts above zero can exaggerate small differences, and a pie chart must show sectors proportional to the data. Frequency diagrams need equal class widths, or frequencies must be scaled by width.

### Choosing an average

Use the mean for symmetric data with no extremes, the median when a few extreme values would distort the mean (such as salaries), and the mode for categories (such as favourite colours). Always consider the range too, since identical averages can hide very different spreads.

**Key term — Range:** The difference between the largest and smallest values, giving a simple measure of how spread out the data is.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: Charts and graphs">
  A scatter diagram shows points rising left to right. What type of correlation is this?

  Positive correlation.

  **Answer:** Positive correlation.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Common mistakes">
  - **Reading a truncated bar chart at face value** If the y-axis does not start at zero, bars exaggerate differences — check the scale before comparing.
  - **Finding the median without ordering the data** Always sort the values first; the median is the middle of the ordered list, not the middle of the original list.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="Why might a bar chart with a y-axis starting at 50 be misleading?" hint="Think about how bar lengths compare.">
    Bars look far more different than the values are, because the truncated axis exaggerates small differences.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Find the mean of 4, 7, 9, 12, 18." hint="Add, then divide by 5.">
    Sum = 50; mean = 50 ÷ 5 = 10.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Find the median of 11, 4, 9, 2, 7." hint="Order the data first.">
    Ordered: 2, 4, 7, 9, 11; median = 7.
  </PracticeItem>
  <PracticeItem question="Find the mode and range of 2, 6, 4, 6, 6, 3." hint="Mode is most frequent; range is max minus min.">
    Mode = 6; range = 6 − 2 = 4.
  </PracticeItem>
</PracticeQuestions>
