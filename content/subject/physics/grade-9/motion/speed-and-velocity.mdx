## Speed

Speed tells us how much distance an object covers in a certain time. A runner who covers 100 m in 10 s has a speed of 10 m/s. Most journeys are not perfectly steady, so we usually calculate an average speed for the whole trip.

<Formula label="Speed equation">
  speed = distance ÷ time
</Formula>

<Definition term="average speed">
  Total distance travelled divided by total time taken.
</Definition>

## Velocity

Speed has only a size. Velocity has both a size and a direction. Two cars moving at 20 m/s in opposite directions have the same speed but different velocities. If an object changes direction, its velocity changes even when its speed stays the same.

<Definition term="velocity">
  Speed in a stated direction.
</Definition>

<WorkedExample title="Average speed">
  A cyclist travels 3 km in 10 minutes. Find the average speed in m/s.

  ```maths
  distance = 3 km = 3000 m
  time = 10 min = 600 s
  speed = 3000 ÷ 600 = 5 m/s
  ```

  **Answer:** 5 m/s
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Match your units">
  Convert distance and time before you calculate if the question asks for m/s.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A sprinter runs 200 m in 25 s. Find the speed." answer="200 ÷ 25 = 8 m/s." />
  <PracticeItem question="A bus travels at 15 m/s for 40 s. How far does it go?" answer="distance = speed × time = 15 × 40 = 600 m." />
  <PracticeItem question="How long does it take to walk 120 m at 1.5 m/s?" answer="time = distance ÷ speed = 120 ÷ 1.5 = 80 s." />
  <PracticeItem question="A car travels 60 km in 1 hour, then 30 km in 1 hour. Find its average speed." answer="Total distance is 90 km and total time is 2 hours, so average speed = 45 km/h." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Speed = distance ÷ time.
  - Average speed uses total distance and total time.
  - Velocity is speed in a stated direction.
  - Check the units before calculating.
