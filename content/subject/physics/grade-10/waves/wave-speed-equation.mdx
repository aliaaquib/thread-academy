## Describing a wave

A wave has a repeating shape. The distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave is called the wavelength. The number of waves passing a point each second is the frequency. If you count more waves each second, the frequency is higher.

<Definition term="wavelength">
  The distance between one point on a wave and the same point on the next wave, measured in metres.
</Definition>

<Definition term="frequency">
  The number of waves passing a point each second, measured in hertz (Hz).
</Definition>

## The wave equation

One wave moves forward by one wavelength in one period. If f waves pass each second, the wave travels f wavelengths each second. This gives the wave equation.

<Formula label="Wave speed">
  v = f × λ
</Formula>

Here v is wave speed in m/s, f is frequency in Hz and λ is wavelength in m. The period is the time for one wave, so T = 1 ÷ f.

<WorkedExample title="Sound in air">
  A sound wave has frequency 170 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find its speed.

  ```maths
  v = fλ
  v = 170 × 2
  v = 340 m/s
  ```

  **Answer:** 340 m/s
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Convert first">
  Convert kHz to Hz and cm to m before you use the equation. For example, 3 kHz is 3000 Hz.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 3 m. Find its speed." answer="v = 5 × 3 = 15 m/s." />
  <PracticeItem question="A wave has speed 20 m/s and wavelength 4 m. Find the frequency." answer="f = v ÷ λ = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 Hz." />
  <PracticeItem question="Find the period of a wave with frequency 50 Hz." answer="T = 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02 s." />
  <PracticeItem question="Radio waves travel at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Find the wavelength of a 100 MHz signal." answer="100 MHz = 1.0 × 10⁸ Hz. λ = v ÷ f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁸ = 3 m." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Wavelength is the length of one complete wave.
  - Frequency is the number of waves each second.
  - Wave speed = frequency × wavelength.
  - Convert to hertz and metres first.
</Summary>
