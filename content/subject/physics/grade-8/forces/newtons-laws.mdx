## Newton's first law

An object stays at rest or keeps moving at a steady speed in a straight line unless a resultant force acts on it. A book on a table stays still because the forces on it balance. A passenger jerks forward when a bus stops because their body tends to keep moving.

<Definition term="resultant force">
  The single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on an object.
</Definition>

## Newton's second law

A resultant force makes an object accelerate. A bigger force gives more acceleration, while a bigger mass gives less.

<Formula label="Newton's second law">
  force (N) = mass (kg) × acceleration (m/s²)
</Formula>

## Newton's third law

When object A pushes on object B, object B pushes back on object A with an equal force in the opposite direction. These two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel each other.

<WorkedExample title="Calculate acceleration">
  A trolley of mass 20 kg is pushed with a resultant force of 50 N. Find its acceleration.

  ```maths
  F = ma
  a = F ÷ m
  a = 50 ÷ 20 = 2.5 m/s²
  ```

  **Answer:** 2.5 m/s²
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Balanced does not mean stopped">
  Balanced forces mean no acceleration. The object can be still, or it can keep moving at a steady speed.
</ImportantNote>

<ExamFocus
  items={[
    { good: "The resultant force is zero, so the speed stays constant.", bad: "There are no forces, so it keeps going.", note: "Forces may still act, but they balance." },
  ]}
  mistake="Saying that third-law pairs cancel. They act on different objects."
/>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A 1000 kg car accelerates at 3 m/s². Find the resultant force." answer="F = ma = 1000 × 3 = 3000 N." />
  <PracticeItem question="A 12 N resultant force gives a 4 m/s² acceleration. Find the mass." answer="m = F ÷ a = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 kg." />
  <PracticeItem question="A swimmer pushes water backwards. What does the water do?" answer="It pushes the swimmer forwards with an equal force." />
  <PracticeItem question="A 2 kg box is pulled with 10 N against 4 N of friction. Find its acceleration." answer="Resultant force = 10 − 4 = 6 N, so a = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 m/s²." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - With no resultant force, velocity stays constant.
  - A resultant force causes acceleration: F = ma.
  - Forces come in equal and opposite pairs.
  - Third-law pairs act on different objects.
</Summary>
