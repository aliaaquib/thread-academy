## What is friction?

Friction is a force between two surfaces that slide or try to slide past each other. Even surfaces that look smooth have tiny bumps. These bumps catch on each other and resist movement. Friction always acts in the opposite direction to the movement, so it slows moving objects down.

<Definition term="friction">
  A force that resists movement between two surfaces in contact.
</Definition>

## When friction helps

Friction lets your shoes grip the floor when you walk. Without it, your feet would slide as they do on smooth ice. Bicycle brakes press pads against the wheel, and friction slows the bicycle. Car tyres grip the road so that the car can turn and stop safely.

## When friction causes problems

Friction can wear surfaces away and make moving parts hot. It also means engines need more energy. We reduce friction with a lubricant such as oil, smooth surfaces, or wheels and ball bearings.

<Definition term="lubricant">
  A substance such as oil that reduces friction between surfaces.
</Definition>

<WorkedExample title="Explain a choice of shoe">
  Why do football boots have studs?

  Grass can be slippery, so ordinary flat soles may slide.

  Studs press into the ground and increase the grip.

  **Answer:** Studs increase friction between the boot and the ground, so the player is less likely to slip.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Rubbing creates heat">
  Rub your hands together quickly and they feel warm. Friction transfers some energy to heat.
</ImportantNote>

<ExamFocus
  items={[
    { good: "Oil reduces friction between the moving parts.", bad: "Oil stops friction.", note: "A lubricant reduces friction but does not remove it completely." },
  ]}
  mistake="Saying that friction is always bad. It is useful when we need grip or need to slow down."
/>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="In which direction does friction act on a sliding box?" answer="Opposite to the direction in which the box is moving." />
  <PracticeItem question="Give one way friction is useful on a bicycle." answer="Brake pads use friction to slow the wheel. Tyres also use it to grip the road." />
  <PracticeItem question="Why do people oil a squeaky door hinge?" answer="Oil reduces friction between the moving metal parts, so they move more easily and quietly." />
  <PracticeItem question="A car drives onto an icy road. Explain why it takes longer to stop." answer="Ice is smooth, so there is less friction between the tyres and the road. The force slowing the car is smaller, so stopping takes longer." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Friction resists movement between surfaces.
  - It helps us grip, walk, turn and stop.
  - It can also cause wear and heating.
  - Lubricants and smooth surfaces reduce friction.
</Summary>
