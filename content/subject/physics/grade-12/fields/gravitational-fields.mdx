## What is a gravitational field?

A gravitational field is a region where a mass experiences a force. Field lines show the direction of the force on a small test mass. Near the surface of a planet the lines look almost parallel, so the field is nearly uniform. Further away, the lines point towards the centre, so the field is radial.

<Definition term="gravitational field strength">
  The gravitational force per unit mass at a point, measured in N/kg.
</Definition>

## Newton's law of gravitation

Every pair of masses attracts each other. The force is proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

```maths
F = Gm₁m₂ ÷ r²
g = F ÷ m
g = GM ÷ r²
```

G is the gravitational constant, 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².

<WorkedExample title="Field strength at Earth's surface">
  Earth has mass 6.0 × 10²⁴ kg and radius 6.4 × 10⁶ m. Calculate g at its surface.

  ```maths
  g = GM ÷ r²
  g = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6.0 × 10²⁴) ÷ (6.4 × 10⁶)²
  g ≈ 9.8 N/kg
  ```

  **Answer:** about 9.8 N/kg
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Measure r from the centre">
  For a satellite, r is the planet's radius plus the height above the surface.
</ImportantNote>

  items={[

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A 2.0 kg mass has a weight of 3.2 N on the Moon. Find g." answer="g = F ÷ m = 3.2 ÷ 2.0 = 1.6 N/kg." />
  <PracticeItem question="The distance between two masses doubles. What happens to the gravitational force?" answer="The force becomes one quarter, because F is proportional to 1 ÷ r²." />
  <PracticeItem question="Why is the field close to Earth's surface treated as uniform?" answer="Over a small height, the field lines are nearly parallel and g is almost constant." />
  <PracticeItem question="A satellite orbits at 2R from Earth's centre, where R is Earth's radius. Find g there if g = 9.8 N/kg at the surface." answer="g is proportional to 1 ÷ r². Doubling r gives g = 9.8 ÷ 4 = 2.45 N/kg." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - A gravitational field is a region where masses feel a force.
  - g is the force per unit mass.
  - Force follows an inverse square law.
  - Measure r between centres of mass.
