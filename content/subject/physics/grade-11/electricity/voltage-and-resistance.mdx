## Potential difference

A cell gives energy to the charges in a circuit. Components transfer that energy as the charges pass through them. Potential difference, also called voltage, measures the energy transferred per unit of charge. We measure it with a voltmeter connected in parallel across a component.

<Definition term="potential difference">
  The energy transferred per coulomb of charge between two points, measured in volts (V).
</Definition>

## Resistance

Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes current. For the same potential difference, a higher resistance gives a smaller current.

<Definition term="resistance">
  The ratio of potential difference to current, measured in ohms (Ω).
</Definition>

<Formula label="Resistance equation">
  V = I × R
</Formula>

At constant temperature, the current through a fixed resistor is directly proportional to the potential difference. Its current and voltage graph is therefore a straight line through the origin.

<WorkedExample title="Find resistance from a measurement">
  A resistor has 6.0 V across it and a current of 0.30 A through it. Find its resistance.

  ```maths
  V = IR
  R = V ÷ I
  R = 6.0 ÷ 0.30 = 20 Ω
  ```

  **Answer:** 20 Ω
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Meter positions">
  Put the ammeter in series with the component and the voltmeter in parallel across it.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="A 12 V supply is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. Find the current." answer="I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A." />
  <PracticeItem question="A current of 2 A flows through a 15 Ω resistor. Find the potential difference." answer="V = IR = 2 × 15 = 30 V." />
  <PracticeItem question="A current of 200 mA flows with 5 V across a component. Find the resistance." answer="200 mA = 0.2 A, so R = 5 ÷ 0.2 = 25 Ω." />
  <PracticeItem question="A student doubles the voltage across a filament lamp and the current less than doubles. Explain what happened to its resistance." answer="R = V ÷ I increased. The lamp became hotter, so the metal ions vibrated more and opposed the flow of charge more." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge.
  - Resistance is potential difference divided by current.
  - Use V = IR with volts, amps and ohms.
  - Connect ammeters in series and voltmeters in parallel.
</Summary>
