## Solve one part at a time

Big problems can feel impossible at first. Decomposition means splitting a problem into smaller parts. Each part is easier to think about and solve. When every part works, you can join them to solve the original problem. Teams can also share the work, with each person building a separate part.

<Definition term="decomposition">
  Breaking a problem down into smaller, more manageable parts.
</Definition>

<Definition term="subproblem">
  A smaller problem that forms part of a larger one.
</Definition>

## Decomposing a game

A quiz game may sound like one large task, but it contains smaller ones: storing questions, showing a question, reading an answer, checking the answer, updating the score and showing the result. Each part can be built and tested on its own.

<WorkedExample title="Plan a school website">
  Decompose the task of creating a school website.

  1. Plan the pages that are needed.
  2. Write the content.
  3. Design the layout.
  4. Add photos and links.
  5. Test the website on different devices.

  **Answer:** One large task is now five smaller tasks.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Stop when a task is clear">
  Keep breaking a problem down until each part is small enough to solve directly.
</ImportantNote>
