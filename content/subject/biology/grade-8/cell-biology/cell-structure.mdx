## Parts found in both cells

Animal and plant cells share several parts. The nucleus controls the cell and contains its genetic material. The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves. Most chemical reactions happen in the cytoplasm. Mitochondria release energy during respiration, and ribosomes make proteins.

<Definition term="organelle">
  A structure inside a cell that carries out a specific job.
</Definition>

<Definition term="nucleus">
  The part of a cell that controls its activities and holds DNA.
</Definition>

## Extra parts in plant cells

Plant cells also have a cell wall made of cellulose, which supports the cell. Many have chloroplasts containing chlorophyll, which absorb light for photosynthesis. A large permanent vacuole holds cell sap and helps keep the cell firm.

<WorkedExample title="Identify a cell">
  A cell has a nucleus, a cell wall and chloroplasts. Is it from a plant or an animal?

  Animal cells do not have a cell wall or chloroplasts.

  **Answer:** It is a plant cell.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Not all plant cells are green">
  Root cells are below ground and have no chloroplasts, because they receive no light.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="What does the cell membrane do?" answer="It controls which substances enter and leave the cell." />
  <PracticeItem question="Where are proteins made?" answer="At the ribosomes." />
  <PracticeItem question="Name three parts found in plant cells but not animal cells." answer="Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole." />
  <PracticeItem question="A muscle cell has many mitochondria. Explain why this is useful." answer="Muscle cells need a lot of energy to contract. More mitochondria release more energy through respiration." level="challenge" />

<Summary>
  - Cells contain organelles with specific jobs.
  - The nucleus controls the cell and the membrane controls entry and exit.
  - Mitochondria release energy and ribosomes make proteins.
  - Plant cells also have a wall, vacuole and often chloroplasts.
</Summary>
