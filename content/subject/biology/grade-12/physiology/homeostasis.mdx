## Keeping conditions stable

Cells work best when their surroundings stay within narrow limits. Homeostasis maintains conditions such as temperature, water balance and blood glucose. Receptors detect a change, a control centre processes the information, and effectors respond to return the condition towards normal.

<Definition term="homeostasis">
  The maintenance of a stable internal environment.
</Definition>

<Definition term="negative feedback">
  A control process in which a change triggers a response that reverses the change.
</Definition>

## Body temperature

The hypothalamus monitors blood temperature. When you are too hot, skin arterioles widen and sweat evaporates to transfer heat away. When you are too cold, arterioles narrow, muscles shiver and metabolism increases.

## Blood glucose

After a meal, blood glucose rises. The pancreas secretes insulin, and liver and muscle cells take in glucose and store it as glycogen. If glucose falls, the pancreas secretes glucagon and the liver breaks glycogen down into glucose.

<WorkedExample title="Trace a feedback loop">
  Describe what happens after blood glucose rises.

  Pancreatic cells detect the rise and secrete insulin.

  Insulin makes target cells absorb glucose and convert it to glycogen.

  Blood glucose falls back towards normal, so insulin release decreases.

  **Answer:** The response reverses the original rise.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Spell the hormones carefully">
  Glucagon is a hormone. Glycogen is the storage carbohydrate. Glucose is the blood sugar.
</ImportantNote>
