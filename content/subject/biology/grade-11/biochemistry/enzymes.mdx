## Biological catalysts

Enzymes are proteins that speed up chemical reactions in living things. They lower the activation energy, so reactions can happen quickly at body temperature. An enzyme is not used up, so one enzyme molecule can catalyse many reactions.

<Definition term="enzyme">
  A protein that acts as a biological catalyst.
</Definition>

<Definition term="active site">
  The part of an enzyme where the substrate binds.
</Definition>

## Specificity

The active site has a shape that fits a particular substrate. When the substrate binds, an enzyme-substrate complex forms. In the induced fit model, the active site changes shape slightly to fit more closely. The products are released and the enzyme can be used again.

## Temperature and pH

Raising temperature increases kinetic energy, so more collisions occur between enzymes and substrates. Above the optimum temperature, bonds holding the enzyme's shape break and the active site changes. The enzyme is denatured. A pH far from the optimum can denature an enzyme in the same way.

<WorkedExample title="Explain a temperature graph">
  An enzyme's rate rises up to 40°C, then falls quickly. Explain this.

  Up to 40°C, particles move faster and successful collisions increase.

  Above 40°C, the active site changes shape and the substrate no longer fits.

  **Answer:** The optimum is about 40°C, and the enzyme denatures above it.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Denatured is not killed">
  Enzymes are molecules, so they are not alive. Say that they are denatured.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

  <PracticeItem question="Why is an enzyme specific to one substrate?" answer="Its active site has a complementary shape to that substrate." />
  <PracticeItem question="Why can a small amount of enzyme catalyse many reactions?" answer="The enzyme is not used up and can bind new substrate after products leave." />
  <PracticeItem question="Pepsin works in the stomach at about pH 2. Predict and explain what happens if it moves to pH 8." answer="Its activity falls because the pH disrupts bonds in the protein, changing the active site, so the substrate no longer fits." level="challenge" />
