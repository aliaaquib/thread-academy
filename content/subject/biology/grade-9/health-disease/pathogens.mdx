## What is a pathogen?

Many microorganisms are harmless, but some cause disease. These are called pathogens. Bacteria, viruses, fungi and protists can all be pathogens. A disease that can pass from one person to another is called a transmissible disease. Cholera is caused by a bacterium and influenza by a virus.

<Definition term="pathogen">
  A microorganism that causes disease.
</Definition>

<Definition term="transmissible disease">
  A disease that can pass from one host to another.
</Definition>

## How pathogens spread

Pathogens can spread in air droplets from coughs and sneezes, in contaminated food or water, through direct contact and by vectors such as mosquitoes. Malaria, for example, is spread by mosquitoes. Your body has barriers including skin, stomach acid and mucus, while white blood cells attack pathogens that get inside.

<WorkedExample title="Reduce the spread of cholera">
  Cholera spreads through water contaminated with sewage. Suggest how a town can reduce its spread.

  The pathogen reaches people in dirty water.

  Treating sewage and supplying clean water break this route.

  **Answer:** Keep sewage away from drinking water and treat the water supply.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Antibiotics and viruses">
  Antibiotics kill bacteria. They do not kill viruses, so they cannot cure flu or colds.
</ImportantNote>

<Summary>
  - Pathogens are microorganisms that cause disease.
  - They spread through air, food, water, contact and vectors.
  - Body barriers and white blood cells protect us.
  - Hygiene and clean water reduce transmission.
</Summary>
