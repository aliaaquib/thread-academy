## How plants make food

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants make glucose. Chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light energy. This energy is used to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. Oxygen leaves the leaf through tiny pores called stomata.

<Definition term="photosynthesis">
  The process in which plants use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water.
</Definition>

```maths
carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
```

## Limiting factors

The rate of photosynthesis is limited by light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. If one factor is in short supply, it holds the rate back even when the others are plentiful. Very high temperatures reduce the rate because enzymes are damaged.

<Definition term="limiting factor">
  The factor in shortest supply that restricts the rate of a process.
</Definition>

<WorkedExample title="Read a limiting factor graph">
  The rate rises as light increases, then levels off. Explain the flat part of the graph.

  More light no longer increases the rate, so light is not the limiting factor.

  Something else, such as carbon dioxide or temperature, is now limiting.

  **Answer:** Another factor has become limiting.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="Energy is absorbed">
  Light energy is transferred to chemical energy in glucose. Plants do not make energy from nothing.
</ImportantNote>
