  You know that living things need food for energy, and that plants make their own food using sunlight.
</BeforeYouStart>

## Who eats what?

A food chain shows how energy passes from one living thing to the next. Most food chains start with a green plant, which captures energy from the Sun. Animals then get energy by eating plants or other animals. The arrows in a food chain show the direction in which energy moves.

<Definition term="producer">
  A living thing that makes its own food, such as a green plant.
</Definition>

<Definition term="consumer">
  An animal that gets energy by eating other living things.
</Definition>

## Reading a food chain

Look at this chain:

```maths
grass → rabbit → fox
```

Grass is the producer. The rabbit is the first consumer and the fox is the second consumer. Because the fox eats other animals, it is a predator. The rabbit is its prey.

<WorkedExample title="Predict a change">
  In the chain grass → rabbit → fox, disease kills many rabbits. What may happen to the grass and the foxes?

  Fewer rabbits eat the grass, so the amount of grass may increase.

  Foxes have less food, so the number of foxes may fall.

  **Answer:** Grass may increase and foxes may decrease.
</WorkedExample>

<ImportantNote title="The arrow points to the eater">
  Read the arrow as gives energy to. It does not mean eats.
</ImportantNote>

## Practice

<PracticeQuestions>
  <PracticeItem question="In leaf → caterpillar → bird, name the producer." answer="The leaf, because plants make their own food." />
  <PracticeItem question="In the same chain, name a consumer." answer="The caterpillar or the bird." />
  <PracticeItem question="What do the arrows in a food chain show?" answer="The direction in which energy passes." />
  <PracticeItem question="In leaf → caterpillar → bird, all the birds leave. Explain what may happen to the leaves." answer="Caterpillars increase because fewer are eaten. They eat more leaves, so the number of leaves may fall." level="challenge" />
</PracticeQuestions>

<Summary>
  - Food chains show how energy moves.
  - Producers make food and consumers eat other living things.
  - Arrows show the direction of energy.
  - A change to one organism can affect the others.
</Summary>
