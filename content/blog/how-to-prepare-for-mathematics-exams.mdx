Whether you are sitting GCSE, IGCSE or another school mathematics exam, the preparation that works is the same: master the fundamentals, practise under timed conditions, and fix your error patterns before exam day. This is a realistic plan, not a miracle cure.

## Start with a topic audit

List every chapter in your syllabus and rate yourself honestly: confident, shaky, or lost. Most students find the list splits roughly into thirds. Your study time should go in reverse order — the "lost" third gets the most hours, because that is where the marks are hiding.

Begin with algebra: it underpins everything else. If rearranging equations and factorising feel automatic, every other chapter gets easier.

## The six-week structure

- **Weeks 1–3: rebuild.** Work through your "lost" and "shaky" chapters, one at a time. Use worked examples actively — attempt first, then compare.
- **Weeks 4–5: past papers.** Two full papers per week under timed conditions. Keep an error log of every lost mark.
- **Week 6: targeted repair.** No new papers. Revisit only the error patterns in your log.

## Using past papers properly

A past paper is a diagnostic tool, not a lottery ticket. After marking:

1. Categorise every error: knowledge gap, method slip, or exam technique (time, misreading).
2. Knowledge gaps go back into your rebuild list.
3. Method slips get five similar questions the same day.

This turns each practice paper into a plan for the next one.

## The final week

- Sleep beats cramming. A tired brain cannot do algebra.
- Skim your error log, not the whole textbook.
- Prepare the practical things: calculator (with fresh batteries), pens, ID, the exam timetable.
- The night before, do thirty minutes of easy questions you can already solve. Walk in having *succeeded* at maths recently.
